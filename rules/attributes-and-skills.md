# Attributes and skills (GURPS 4th Edition Lite)

Source: GURPS 4th Edition Lite PDF on your project (copyright 2004 Steve Jackson Games). Page numbers are the printed "GURPS LITE" page numbers, which match the PDF page numbers. This file covers what a skill test needs. Combat rules are left out. Where a rule below is my reading for the engine and not printed in Lite, it is marked **Engine note**.

## 1. Basic attributes and point costs

| Attribute | Abbr. | Cost per level above or below 10 | What it governs | Page |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Strength | ST | 10 points (-10 below 10) | Physical power and bulk. ST-based skills are very rare. | 4, 5, 13 |
| Dexterity | DX | 20 points (-20 below 10) | Agility, coordination, fine motor ability. Controls most athletic, fighting, vehicle and delicate craft skills. | 4, 5, 13 |
| Intelligence | IQ | 20 points (-20 below 10) | Brainpower: knowledge, creativity, reasoning, perception, willpower. Controls mental skills, sciences and social interaction. | 4, 5, 13 |
| Health | HT | 10 points (-10 below 10) | Energy, vitality, resistance. HT-based skills are governed by physical fitness. | 4, 5, 13 |

- A score of 10 in any attribute is free and is the human average. Higher scores cost points, lower scores give points back. (p. 4)
- Cost of an attribute = (score - 10) x cost per level. ST 12 costs 20. DX 8 costs -40. This is the arithmetic from p. 4; Lite does not print it as a formula.
- Most characters have attributes in the 1 to 20 range and most normal humans are 8 to 12. Scores above 20 need the GM's permission. 1 is the minimum for a human. (p. 4)
- Descriptive bands: 6 or less crippling, 7 poor, 8 or 9 below average, 10 average, 11 or 12 above average, 13 or 14 exceptional, 15 or more amazing. (p. 4 and p. 5)
- Raising an attribute later costs the point-cost difference between the old and new score. (p. 17)

### Controlling attribute
Each skill is based on one of the four basic attributes, and the skill level is calculated from that attribute. A higher attribute makes every skill based on it better. (p. 13)

### Secondary traits a skill test uses
Only these two matter for skill tests. Both are set by IQ and can be raised or lowered by adjusting IQ. (p. 6)

| Trait | Value | Used in | Page |
|---|---|---|---|
| Will | Equal to IQ | Resisting psychological stress. Skills that coerce or interrogate set a Quick Contest of the skill against the target's Will (Influence skills, Interrogation). | 6, 15 |
| Perception (Per) | Equal to IQ | The GM's Sense roll is made against Per. Stealth is contested against the sentinel's Perception. | 6, 17 |

Hit Points, Fatigue Points, Basic Speed, Basic Move, Dodge and damage are not used by a skill test and are left out. (Basic Lift, p. 5, is also left out.)

### Modifier that applies to any skill roll
Using the "off" hand for anything significant gives -4 to skill. It does not apply to things normally done with the off hand. (p. 5)

## 2. The success roll a skill test makes

- Roll 3d (three six-sided dice). The test succeeds if the total is less than or equal to the number that governs the action, most often a skill or attribute. (p. 2)
- A roll of 3 or 4 is always a success. A roll of 17 or 18 is always a failure, whatever the score. (p. 2)
- Modifiers change the target number, not the dice total. Bonuses help and penalties hurt. They are cumulative unless stated otherwise. Example from the book: Lockpicking 9, -5 in the dark, +10 for a primitive lock gives 9 - 5 + 10 = 14. (p. 2)
- Base skill is the level on the character sheet. Effective skill is base skill plus or minus the modifiers for the task. You may not attempt a success roll if effective skill is less than 3, unless it is a defense roll. (p. 2)
- Margin of success is effective skill minus the roll. Margin of failure is the roll minus effective skill. (p. 2)
- Critical success: 3 or 4 always. 5 if effective skill is 15 or more. 6 if effective skill is 16 or more. (p. 2)
- Critical failure: 18 always. 17 if effective skill is 15 or less (otherwise an ordinary failure). Any roll 10 or more above effective skill, such as 16 on a skill of 6. (p. 2)
- Quick Contest of two success rolls: if one succeeds and the other fails, that one wins. If both succeed, the larger margin of success wins. If both fail, the smaller margin of failure wins. A tie means nobody won. (p. 3)

## 3. Skill difficulty levels

GURPS Lite uses three difficulty levels. There is no Very Hard level in this book. If you want Very Hard in the engine, it has to come from another source, not from Lite. (p. 13)

| Level | Code in skill list | Meaning in Lite | Default penalty from controlling attribute | Page |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Easy | E | Things anyone could do reasonably well after a short learning period | -4 | 13 |
| Average | A | Most combat skills, mundane job skills, practical social and survival skills | -5 | 13 |
| Hard | H | Skills that need intensive formal study or training | -6 | 13 |

Skill type is written as attribute/difficulty, for example "IQ/A". E, A and H stand for Easy, Average and Hard. (p. 13)

Some skills are marked "/TL" and are learned at a specific tech level. Always note the TL when you write down such a skill. (p. 13) The Technology Level rules (p. 7) are outside this file.

## 4. Relative skill level and the points-to-level table

Lite expresses a skill level relative to its controlling attribute: "Attribute-1", "Attribute+0" and so on. With DX 12, Attribute-1 is 11, Attribute+0 is 12 and Attribute+1 is 13. (p. 13)

**Skill level = controlling attribute + relative skill level.**

Skill Cost Table, as printed (p. 13). The cells are character points. A dash means that level cannot be bought at that difficulty.

| Final skill level (relative) | Easy | Average | Hard |
|---|---|---|---|
| Attribute-3 | - | - | - |
| Attribute-2 | - | - | 1 |
| Attribute-1 | - | 1 | 2 |
| Attribute+0 | 1 | 2 | 4 |
| Attribute+1 | 2 | 4 | 8 |
| Attribute+2 | 4 | 8 | 12 |
| Attribute+3 | 8 | 12 | 16 |
| Extra +1 | +4 | +4 | +4 |

"Extra +1" means each further level beyond Attribute+3 costs 4 more points. (p. 13)

### The same table read the other way: points to relative level

| Points spent | Easy | Average | Hard |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | +0 | -1 | -2 |
| 2 | +1 | +0 | -1 |
| 4 | +2 | +1 | +0 |
| 8 | +3 | +2 | +1 |
| 12 | +4 (Engine note) | +3 | +2 |
| 16 | +5 (Engine note) | +4 (Engine note) | +3 |

- The cells marked Engine note follow from "Extra +1 = +4 points" on p. 13. The printed table stops at Attribute+3 and does not list them.
- **Engine note:** Lite prints costs only at the rows above. It does not say what level 3 or 5 points buy. The simple reading is the highest row whose cost is at or below the points spent. Confirm this before building on it.
- Improving a skill later costs the difference between the cost of the current level and the cost of the new level. (p. 17)
- Lite's Easy column matches the first four rows of the Average column shifted by one level, and the Hard column is shifted by one more. The engine can store one cost ladder and shift it by difficulty. This is an observation from the table, not a printed rule.

## 5. Defaults

A default lets someone use a skill they have not studied. It applies to skills that everybody can do "a little bit". (p. 13)

| Rule | Detail | Page |
|---|---|---|
| General default | A skill defaults to its controlling attribute at -4 if Easy, -5 if Average, -6 if Hard. | 13 |
| No default | Some skills have none ("Default: None"). You cannot attempt them untrained. Example given: Karate. | 13 |
| Rule of 20 | If a skill defaults to a basic attribute above 20, treat that attribute as 20 when working out the default. | 13 |
| Who gets a default | Only people from a society where the skill is known may roll a default. Example: a medieval knight gets no default roll for scuba gear. | 13 |
| Skill list entries | Each entry gives the default attribute and penalty. The entry overrides the general rule where they differ (see exceptions below). | 13 |
| Weapon skill families | Melee Weapon and Missile Weapon skills are DX-based and default to DX-4 if Easy, DX-5 if Average, DX-6 if Hard. | 15 |
| Vehicle skills | Default to DX at -4 (Easy), -5 (Average) or -6 (Hard). Motor vehicles also default to IQ at similar penalties. | 17 |

**Defaults to other skills:** the Lite skill list gives defaults to attributes only. No entry defaults to another skill. Two entries use a related skill or attribute for a roll but this is not a default: Jumping uses the higher of Jumping or DX (p. 15), and Swimming uses the higher of Swimming or HT for some rolls (p. 17). If the engine needs skill-to-skill defaults it will need a source other than Lite.

**Entries that differ from the general rule** (listed in the skill table below):

- Throwing is Average but defaults to DX-3 (p. 17).
- Sex Appeal is Average but defaults to HT-3 (p. 15).
- Physician/TL is Hard but defaults to IQ-7 (p. 16).
- Computer Programming/TL, Engineer/TL (both Hard) and Karate (Hard) have no default (pp. 14, 15). Brawling and Jumping (both Easy) also have no default (pp. 14, 15).

## 7. Build notes for the TypeScript model

- Store one cost ladder per difficulty, as in section 4. Compute skill level as controlling attribute plus relative level.
- Store each skill as: name, controlling attribute, difficulty, default (attribute and penalty, or none), tech-level flag, family or sub-skill flag. The table in section 6 holds all of those fields.
- Add a combat flag now so the first slice can skip those skills and later work can pick them up.
- Open questions for you: whether to add Very Hard from outside Lite, what to do for point totals between table rows, and whether TL rules (p. 7) belong in the first slice.
